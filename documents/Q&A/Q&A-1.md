#Q&A


##	What is Padel Up?
Padel Up helps padel players find games, create games, invite players, join open games, and coordinate with other players. It is designed around real game availability, player level, location preferences, and clear approval/consent rules.

##	What does the app do?
The app lets registered players create games, create join requests, discover matching games, filter by location/time/rating, join games, approve players, receive notifications, and manage their own padel activity.

##	What does the app not do?
The app does not book courts automatically, does not guarantee that a game will happen, and does not force players into games without their consent. Court booking and final real-world coordination remain the responsibility of the players.

##	What is the difference between a Game and a Request?
A Game is a concrete padel session with a specific date, time, and location, usually after a court is booked. A Request is more flexible: a player is saying “I want to play” within a time/location range and is looking for matching players or games.

##	Can a Request cover multiple days?
Yes. A Request may span multiple days because no court is booked yet. The selected time range applies to each day in that date range.

##	Can a Game cover multiple days?
No. A Game represents a booked or planned session at a specific time and is treated as a single-day event.

##	Who is the organizer?
The organizer is the player who created the Game or Request. The organizer is automatically confirmed as part of it and manages the player list, approvals, edits, and cancellation/archive actions.

##	What can the organizer do?
The organizer can edit game details, approve or reject join requests, add players, remove players, re-approve rejected players, cancel/archive the game, and convert a Request into a Game.

##	What can regular players do?
Players can browse visible games and requests, apply to join, leave games they joined, create their own requests, and confirm or reject participation when their consent is required.

##	Can guests see games?
No. Only registered and logged-in users can view games, requests, and history.

##	How does joining a game work?
A player requests to join. The request becomes pending until the organizer approves it. Once approved, the player becomes confirmed unless additional consent is required.

##	Why does a player sometimes need to give consent?
Consent is required when the organizer adds a player directly or when important game details change, such as date, time, or location. This prevents players from being committed to changed plans without agreement.

##	What happens if a player rejects consent?
The player is marked as rejected for that game. The organizer can later remove the player or re-approve them if needed.

##	When does a game become closed?
A game closes automatically when it has 4 fully confirmed players. Fully confirmed means the player status is confirmed and no further consent is required.

##	Can people still join after a game is closed?
No. Once a game is closed, new join requests are disabled.

##	What happens to pending players when a game closes?
When a game closes with 4 confirmed players, extra pending or rejected players are removed from that game and notified.

##	Can a closed game reopen?
Yes. If confirmed players drop below 4, the game can reopen automatically.

##	What is game history?
History contains past or archived games and requests. Archived items are generally read-only, but an organizer may reopen an archived game by editing it to a future date.

##	Which games are visible to whom?
Active games and requests are visible to all logged-in users. Closed and archived games are visible only to participants. History shows only games or requests where the current user participated.

##	What is “Just My Games”?
“Just My Games” filters the list to only games and requests where the current user is a participant or organizer.

##	How do filters work?
Filters narrow games by date, time, location, rating range, game type, and “Just My Games.” The app first fetches matching candidates, then applies user-specific visibility rules so users only see what they are allowed to see.

##	How does location filtering work?
Users choose location preferences such as regions or specific clubs. A game matches if it belongs to any selected club or region. If no location filter is selected, location does not restrict results.

##	How does rating filtering work?
Games can define a required player rating range. A player must fit the game’s rating requirements to join. Search filters also use overlap logic, so games are shown when their rating range overlaps the selected filter range.

##	Why can I see a game but not join it?
Possible reasons include: your rating does not match the game’s requirements, you are already involved, or another rule prevents joining.

##	How are conflicts resolved?
Only real Games create time conflicts, because they represent booked sessions. Requests do not create conflicts. Before approval or consent, the app checks whether the player is already confirmed in an overlapping closed Game.

##	What counts as a conflict?
A conflict exists when two Games overlap by both date and time. Date overlap alone is not enough, and time overlap alone is not enough.

##	What happens if a conflict is found?
The conflicting approval or consent is blocked, and the affected player receives a notification explaining why.

##	What happens when a game closes and overlaps with other active games?
When a game closes, the system checks confirmed players against overlapping active games. Players may be removed from conflicting active games, and organizers may have their overlapping active games archived. Affected users are notified.

##	Can a Request become a Game?
Yes. The organizer can convert a Request into a Game after details become concrete, such as choosing a single location and date.

##	What happens when a Request is converted into a Game?
If it already has 4 confirmed players, one location, one date, and valid time details, it can close automatically. Otherwise, players are asked to confirm consent again because the plan became more specific.

##	What are recommendations?
Recommendations notify users when an open Game and an open Request appear compatible by date, time, rating, and location. They help players discover good matches without manually searching everything. Recommendations also simplify and accelerate games closing process.

##	Do push notifications always arrive?
In-app notifications are the reliable source. Native push notifications are attempted when available, but device delivery can depend on phone settings, tokens, and platform behavior.

##	When are users notified?
Users may be notified when they are added to a game, when someone requests to join, when a request is approved or rejected, when consent is needed, when a game closes or reopens, when a conflict is resolved, or when a recommendation is found.

##	Does the app send notifications for my own actions?
No. Users generally do not receive notifications for actions they performed themselves.

##	How does onboarding work?
A new user signs in, sets their current location using GPS or manual input, and the app finds nearby clubs. Those clubs are added to the user’s filter so they can start discovering games quickly.

##	Is my phone or email visible to everyone?
The app protects sensitive details in presentation. Basic player information may be visible to logged-in users, while sensitive contact details are controlled and not treated as public guest content.

##	Who can delete or archive a game?
Only the organizer can cancel/archive their own game or request.

##	Who can change game details?
Only the organizer can edit the main game/request details. When important details change, existing players may need to consent again.

##	Why does the app use approval instead of instant joining?
Approval gives the organizer control over the final group, while still allowing multiple players to express interest before the game is full.

##	What happens if several players request the last spot?
Pending requests do not count as confirmed players. The organizer can approve players until 4 are fully confirmed. Once the game closes, extra pending/rejected players are removed and notified.
